# Peldaño — Customer Personas

**v1 · 26 Oct 2026** · Built from ten weeks of User-role research (main source: W9 Brief, Chapter 8, USER lens) and corrected by the first real interview (26 Oct 2026).
These personas are who the persona tests impersonate and who marketing targets. They are versioned: when reality corrects them, update the version and log the change at the bottom.

> **Data rule (Security Floor):** both personas are invented composites. No real person's name or personal data appears here. The real interviewee is cited only as evidence.

Evidence tags: **[INTERVIEW]** said by a real person · **[RESEARCH]** from W9 research · **[HYPOTHESIS]** not yet validated — to test in week 13.

## Persona 1 — Iván, the worker who absorbs the loss

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Iván (invented composite) |
| **Age** | 22 |
| **Context** | Lives in Iztapalapa, CDMX. Finished prepa. Two years of informal work: deliveries for apps in the evenings (~25 h/week, some months under the IMSS threshold, so no formal record) [RESEARCH]. Now applying to small cafés, restaurants and shops for a steady job. Uses WhatsApp for everything; downloads few apps; gives up quietly when a form asks too much. |
| **The need, in his words** | "Uno va con la intención de conseguir trabajo, le echas ganas y al final ni te hablan." [INTERVIEW — worker, 26 Oct 2026, paraphrased into the persona's voice] · "Tengo dos años trabajando y me dicen que no tengo experiencia." [RESEARCH — W9 rejection moment] |
| **What he does today** | Works a full trial shift — sometimes unpaid — and is never called back. Leaves with nothing to show the next employer [INTERVIEW]. Repeats it at the next place, or uses a *palanca* through a relative, or stays on the apps [RESEARCH]. Each unpaid trial costs him a day of earnings plus fare. |
| **What he'd pay** | **Nothing.** The worker pays zero (Verdict, Gate 2). He is the one who already absorbs the cost. |
| **What would make him quit** | Having to create an account with a long form or email · the record looking like a rating that could hurt him · the business owner refusing or forgetting to sign · showing the record and the employer ignoring it — then it's "otro papel que no sirve" [HYPOTHESIS] · the app asking for data he doesn't want to give. |
| **What success looks like for him** | After his next trial day, he has a signed record on his phone, and an employer who signed the Acuerdo counts it as experience. |
| **Still unknown (ask in week 13)** | (a) Pesos lost per unpaid trial, incl. fare · (b) how many trials before getting hired · excluded or exiter: "Si mañana te ofrecen un trabajo formal que paga lo mismo, ¿lo tomas?" [W9 test] · trusted channel: how he hears about jobs (WhatsApp, Facebook, family). |

**Persona-test prompt (copy into a fresh chat):**
> You are Iván, 22, from Iztapalapa. You finished prepa and have delivered for food apps for two years. You're looking for a steady job at a café or restaurant. You've done trial days where nobody called you back. You use WhatsApp all day but you don't trust apps that ask for a lot. You read fast but skip anything long. If something confuses you, you don't ask — you just close it. I'll show you screens one by one. Try to get a signed record of your trial day, AS IVÁN. Narrate out loud where you hesitate, what you don't understand, and where you'd quit.

## Persona 2 — Rosa, the manager who runs trial days (observer and possible signer)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Rosa (invented composite) |
| **Age** | 38 |
| **Context** | Owns and runs a small café in the Narvarte area, CDMX, with 4–6 employees, high turnover in barista/waiter roles. Hires informally — word of mouth, a sign in the window, Facebook. Tests every candidate with a trial day before deciding [HYPOTHESIS, based on the interview finding that unpaid trials happen in small businesses, not big chains]. Busy, on her feet all day, does admin from her phone. |
| **The need, in her words** | [HYPOTHESIS — not interviewed] "No puedo contratar a alguien sin verlo trabajar. Pero no tengo tiempo de estar haciendo papeles." · "Lo que me cuesta es cuando entra alguien y a la semana se va." |
| **What she does today** | Runs a trial day, decides in her head, and doesn't call back the ones she doesn't pick [INTERVIEW — from the worker's side]. Writes nothing down. When hiring, trusts a recommendation from someone she knows over any CV [RESEARCH — W9, JCF works as a "palanca factory"]. |
| **What she'd pay** | **Unknown — probably nothing at first.** [HYPOTHESIS] Possible value for her: lower turnover from hiring people with proven trial days at other businesses; looking good to candidates. Who pays is an open question in the Charter. |
| **What would make her quit** | Signing takes more than 2 minutes · anything that looks like a legal document admitting she had someone work unpaid — a real exposure [Verdict, Risk #1] · having to install an app or create a full account · feeling the record could be used against her. |
| **What success looks like for her** | She signs a short record in under 2 minutes after the trial day. Later, she accepts a candidate's signed records from other businesses as experience — and that hire stays. |
| **Still unknown (ask in week 13)** | Would she sign an Acuerdo that N signed trial days count as experience? · Does she pay her trial days? · What would make signing a record feel safe, not risky? · Would she write detailed observations, or only a few words? |

**Persona-test prompt (copy into a fresh chat):**
> You are Rosa, 38, owner of a small café in Narvarte, CDMX. You test every candidate with a trial day. You're always busy and do everything from your phone between customers. You don't want to sign anything that sounds legal or that admits someone worked for free. You have no patience for long forms. I'll show you screens one by one. Try to review and sign the record of a candidate's trial day, AS ROSA. Narrate out loud where you hesitate, what worries you, and where you'd stop.
